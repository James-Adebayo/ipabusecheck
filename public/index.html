    How it works

    A lookup takes a few seconds and needs no account.

    1. 1

      Enter an address

      Paste any public IPv4 or IPv6 address, for example one from your server logs or a suspicious email header. Your own address is filled in for you.

    2. 2

      We check the reports

      The address is checked against AbuseIPDB, a shared database where network owners report attacks and spam.

    3. 3

      Read the result

      You get a confidence score from 0 to 100, who owns the address, and the most recent reports with their categories.

    How to read the score

    The abuse confidence score reflects how many people reported the address, how recently, and how reliable those reporters are. Treat it as a strong hint rather than proof.

    0 to 24: looks clean

    Few or no reports. Normal traffic is expected, but an unreported address is not automatically safe, especially if it is new or only just started attacking.

    25 to 74: suspicious

    Some reports exist. Read the comments and dates. Rate-limit or challenge the traffic (CAPTCHA, extra verification) instead of blocking it outright.

    75 to 100: likely abusive

    Many recent reports from several reporters. Blocking at your firewall, web server or tools like fail2ban is reasonable, and so is refusing signups or payments from it.

    Common types of abuse

    Each report is tagged with one or more categories. These are the ones you will see most.

    Brute-force
    Repeated login guesses against a service, usually with lists of common passwords.
    SSH
    Attacks aimed at SSH servers, such as login attempts for users like root or oracle.
    Port scan
    Probing many ports to find open services that can be attacked later.
    Web app attack
    Attempts to exploit websites, such as vulnerable plugins, admin pages or login forms.
    SQL injection
    Sending crafted input to a site in an attempt to read or change its database.
    DDoS attack
    Flooding a server with traffic to knock it offline.
    Phishing
    Fake pages or emails built to steal passwords and payment details.
    Email and web spam
    Unwanted messages and comments, often with links to scams or malware.
    Open proxy
    A server that forwards traffic for anyone, which attackers use to hide their real address.
    Bad web bot
    Automated crawlers that ignore rules and scrape or hammer a site.

    When this helps

    Anywhere an unknown address touches something you run.

    Reviewing server logs

    Found repeated failed logins or odd requests? Check where they came from before deciding to block them.

    Spotting fraud

    Check the address behind a suspicious signup, order or payment before approving it.

    Investigating email

    Look up the sending address in a spam or phishing message to see if it has a history.

    Securing your site

    Decide which addresses to add to a firewall blocklist, and which only need rate limiting.

    Questions

    Short answers to the things people ask most.

    Why only one lookup per day?

    The reports come from a third-party database that limits how many checks this site can make. One lookup per visitor per day keeps the tool free and available for everyone.

    Does a score of 0 mean the address is safe?

    No. It means nobody has reported it recently. New attackers, and addresses that only target a few sites, can go unreported for a long time.

    Why is a big company's address flagged?

    Cloud and hosting providers rent addresses to thousands of customers, and one compromised customer can get a shared address reported. Look at the report dates and comments before blocking an entire provider.

    What if the address is shared, like a VPN or office network?

    Many people can sit behind one public address, so a report may describe just one of them. The score is less meaningful for these, so review the comments rather than relying on the number.

    What does "allowlisted" mean?

    AbuseIPDB marks some well-known, trusted addresses as allowlisted. Their score is usually kept low even if a few reports come in.

    Is the address I search saved?

    No. To enforce the daily limit, the site keeps your own IP address and the time of your last lookup for about 24 hours. The address you searched for is not stored.

    Why do I only see reports from the last 90 days?

    Lookups cover the past 90 days, so older incidents drop out. An address that was abusive years ago but is quiet now will usually look clean.
